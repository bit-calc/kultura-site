Russian manicure in Buenos Aires: where to get it, prices and answers to common questions

What people ask most before booking: where to get it, what it costs, how long it lasts, whether it hurts and how to choose.

A Russian manicure leaves a clean outline around the nail, colour right up to the cuticle and neat hands for weeks. This guide brings together the questions we hear most: first the concrete ones — where, how much and with whom — and then the general ones, so you know what to expect before you book.

What is a Russian manicure?

It’s a dry manicure done with an e-file and fine bits, with no soaking. The technician removes dead skin and the cuticle completely and leaves a clean, even outline around the nail; living skin isn’t touched. With the cuticle gone, the polish can go on almost flush with the skin.

Are a Russian manicure, an e-file manicure and a SMART manicure the same thing?

They usually name the same technique. “Russian manicure” is the name it’s known by worldwide; “e-file” or “dry” describe how the work is done; and it’s also called a SMART manicure.

How is it different from a traditional manicure and from gel polish?

In a traditional manicure the cuticle is softened with water or remover, pushed back and trimmed with nippers. In a Russian manicure everything is done dry, with an e-file, and the cuticle is removed completely. Gel polish isn’t a different technique but the coating: a Russian manicure almost always ends with gel polish.

What is strengthening?

A layer that levels the nail and makes it tougher; the colour goes on top of that base. It helps the nail hold its shape and suits nails that are thin or break.

Does a Russian manicure hurt?

It shouldn’t hurt. It’s precision work: the right bits, light pressure, and only dead skin is removed. If you feel heat or burning, tell your technician straight away.

How long does it last and how often should you have it done?

The polish usually lasts three to four weeks. Because it sits right up to the cuticle, regrowth takes a while to show; that’s also the usual interval for a refill.

How do you look after it at home?

  • Cuticle oil every day.
  • Gloves for cleaning and washing up.
  • Don’t peel the polish off yourself. Removal is done in the studio.

What nail art can you have?

French, stamping, sliders, ombré, chrome and hand-painted art, from minimal nude to bold designs. Nail art on all nails is added to the manicure and costs from ARS 17,500. If you already have an idea, send us the photo on WhatsApp to check the price.

What if I want more length?

That’s what gel extensions are for: the length and shape you choose. They cost from ARS 92,000 and take up to three and a half hours; a refill costs from ARS 55,000.

Do you do pedicures too?

Yes: the Russian SMART pedicure, with or without foot care and polish. With foot care and polish it costs from ARS 77,500 and takes up to two and a half hours; with foot care and no polish, from ARS 64,500; with no foot care or polish, from ARS 55,000.

How do you choose where to get a Russian manicure?

  • Look at close-up photos of real work. The cuticle line should look clean, and the colour even and right against the skin.
  • Ask how much time they book. It’s precision work and takes longer than a regular manicure.
  • Ask for the final price before you start. Strengthening, length and nail art change it.
  • Say what condition your nails are in. If they’re thin or break, ask about strengthening.
