Russian manicure: colour right up to the cuticle

An e-file technique that keeps hands looking immaculate for weeks. How it works and why people choose it.

A Russian manicure is a dry e-file technique: no soaking, fine diamond bits. It’s also called a SMART manicure. The technician pushes the skin back, lifts and fully removes the dead cuticle from the nail plate and refines the area around the nail into a clean, even outline. Living skin is left untouched.

Why the colour sits so close

With the cuticle removed, gel polish can go on almost flush with the skin. Regrowth takes longer to show, and your hands look as if you’ve just left the studio.

Why people choose it

  • A clean outline. No hangnails or ragged skin around the nail.
  • Looks fresh for longer. Gel polish usually lasts three to four weeks.
  • A smooth, strong surface. Strengthening levels the nail and helps it hold its shape.
  • No water. The nail doesn’t swell, so the polish adheres better.

Where the technician’s hands matter

It’s precision work: the right bits, a light touch, and only dead skin is removed. It shouldn’t hurt; if you feel heat or stinging, tell your technician straight away.

How to make it last

  • Cuticle oil every day.
  • Gloves for cleaning and washing up.
  • A refill after three to four weeks. Don’t peel the polish off yourself.
