Russian manicure vs a regular gel manicure

Gel polish is the coating; a Russian manicure is how the nail is prepared. What changes in the result.

They’re often compared as if they were two items on the same menu, but they describe different things. Gel polish is a coating cured under a lamp. A Russian manicure is a technique for preparing the nail and cuticle. They go together: a Russian manicure almost always ends with gel polish.

How the nail is prepared

  • Traditional manicure. The cuticle is softened with water or remover, pushed back and trimmed with nippers.
  • Russian manicure. It’s done dry, with an e-file and fine bits: dead skin and the cuticle are removed completely, leaving a clean, even outline. Living skin isn’t touched.

What you notice in the result

  • Colour sits closer to the skin. With the cuticle removed, the polish is applied practically flush.
  • Regrowth takes longer to show. So hands look freshly done for longer.
  • No water. The nail doesn’t swell and the polish adheres better.
  • An even surface. Strengthening levels the nail and helps it keep its shape.

What to know beforehand

  • It takes longer. At KULTURA a Russian manicure with strengthening and polish takes up to two and a half hours.
  • It depends on the technician’s hand. It’s precision work: the right bits, light pressure, and only dead skin is removed. It shouldn’t hurt; if you feel heat or burning, say so straight away.
  • The price. Because of the time and technique it usually costs more than a plain gel polish. At KULTURA, from ARS 64,500 with strengthening and polish.

Which one suits you

  • You want neat hands for three to four weeks: a Russian manicure.
  • Your nails are thin or break: a Russian manicure with strengthening, which adds a tougher layer.
  • You just want to change colour often: a regular gel polish may be enough.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Russian manicure the same as gel polish?

No. Gel polish is the coating; a Russian manicure is the e-file technique that prepares the nail and cuticle before polishing.

How long does a Russian manicure last?

The polish usually lasts three to four weeks.

Does a Russian manicure hurt?

It shouldn’t hurt. If you feel heat or burning, tell your technician straight away.
